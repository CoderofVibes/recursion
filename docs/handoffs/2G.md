## The choices made

- **Attitude scheme.** Half kicks of the angular momentum around the free rigid body split into exact rotations about its principal axes (x for dt/2, y for dt/2, z for dt, y for dt/2, x for dt/2). Why: it is second order and time-symmetric like the translational scheme it sits inside, keeps the quaternion's length and the inertial angular momentum to rounding, has a bounded energy error with no drift, and reduces exactly to the planar scheme (θ += ω·dt) for a spin about a principal z axis, so planar flight is unchanged. Measured torque-free at 0.05 rad/s and dt = 1 s over 10⁶ s: energy within 5×10⁻⁶ (triaxial) and 4×10⁻⁵ (axisymmetric) of its initial value, bounded and falling 4× per halved step; inertial angular momentum within 10⁻¹²; quaternion length within 10⁻¹² of 1 [DEMONSTRATED: `src/sim/rigid-body-3d.test.ts` asserts the 1 s figures over 4×10⁵ s].
- **Hull shape.** A union of convex prisms: a convex outline in the body x–y plane extruded between two heights. Why: a box is a prism, the planar footprints carry over exactly, and the distance from a point to a prism is exact. Where a pass stays inside a prism's height the planar clearance code is used unchanged, so every planar contact result is bit-identical; elsewhere a fixed-count search is used.
- **Roll.** Held. The player steers yaw and pitch; a pointing target turns the body axis onto its direction by the smallest rotation from the attitude the ship has at that moment (`quat.pointAlong`). The game layer and the model's forecasts use the same function, so the ship flies the attitude that was forecast.
- **Measurements.** Bearings as azimuth and elevation with per-component sigmas; attitude fixes as rotation vectors; both reduce exactly to the planar values.
- **Angular velocity** in the body frame, as the gyros sense it.
- **RCS layout.** 1E's five nozzles per pod, taken literally: every torque is a set of couples with no net force, and sideways translation does not turn the ship. Jet 1 of `rcs-forward-left` and `rcs-aft-right` therefore pushes the opposite way to 1C's planar layout; yaw authority is ±3,600 N·m (1C: −1,600 / +4,000).
- **Attitude controller** [ASSUMED, in the data file]: slew 0.008 rad/s (star trackers lose their solution at 0.01), attitude deadband 0.002 rad, rate deadband 0.0004 rad/s. A hold at rest costs no propellant (2B's cost about 12 kg/h); a 180° turn settles in 420 s for 0.5 kg (2B: 15–29 kg, peaking at 0.18 rad/s). This answers 2F's request 3.
- **Separate noise streams** for the new measurement components (`sensors.<id>/3d`), so the planar components of every measurement draw the same numbers as before.
- **Arrival in the binary's plane.** The ship leaves it when the player flies it out. Out of the plane from the start: Alpha's asteroids, the ring's thickness, the sun.

## The model's singular-matrix failure (2D request 2)

- **Reproduced** exactly on synthetic records shaped like the report (three short attempts, the first ending inside a burn), live and after a reload, and in the real game on the committed tree.
- **Cause.** The staged global fit starts from the first quarter of each arc's time span. For an arc of three or four fixes that is one fix, at the arc's epoch: a position and no velocity, and ship states have no prior, so the normal matrix had two exactly zero rows. The record stays in Black Box, so every later fit hit it again. The reload and the burn mattered only because the fix taken during the burn became the arc's third.
- **Fix.** No stage cuts an arc below its first three range fixes; an arc needs three range fixes at different times. An attempt the model cannot use is left out with a stated reason and the rest are fitted; a global fit that cannot be solved holds the priors and keeps the local fits; a failed call leaves the state unchanged; thrust never runs past the end of its record. Same family, also fixed: an arc epoch after its first measurement, coasting fixes averaged across a short burn, a NaN measurement reported as converged.
- **What the player is told.** `report()` carries `coverage[i].used` and the reason, and `fitFailure`. The model's `lastError` stays empty, so the console must read those to show it (request to 2D below).

- The wide pass: at 1,100–1,650 km from Beta the ship goes round the outside of the ring and still leaves at 50–64 m/s.

**How many runs a first-time player is likely to need** [ASSUMED: reasoning from the numbers above and 2F's forecast study, not a playtest]
- A blind slingshot needs a pass 680–1,650 km from Beta from a burn the Newtonian model aims hundreds of kilometres wrong: 2% of blind burns qualify, and those that do go through the ring more often than not. Expect the first one or two runs to end at Beta, in the ring, or bound to the binary; each leaves tracking data.
- The law can be learned at Alpha without dying: two dives (8 h each) and a held-out test, as in D8. That is one run if the player thinks of it, two or three if they find out by missing.
- With the law adopted, the forecast reaches through the pass about 10 h after the burn (2F), leaving 20 h to trim the pass distance and the height at the ring.
- So: three to five runs for a first-time player; one, flown carefully, for a player who has done it before. A9 holds: the ring is as dense on run ten as on run one, and a pass flown without tracking is still lost 6 times in 10.
