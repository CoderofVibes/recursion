**Integrator and invariants:**
- [ASSUMED] Fixed-step kick–drift–kick velocity Verlet, as 1B recommended; the same scheme on (θ, ω) for rotation; thrust constant in the body frame per step, evaluated at θₖ and θₖ₊₁. Reason: symplectic for gravity, one force evaluation per step, exact for constant torque, second order with turning thrust.
- [DEMONSTRATED: `invariants.test.ts`] Isolated source, 2×10⁶ s (≈ 25 radial periods) at dt = 1 s: relative energy error ≤ 6.9×10⁻⁸ and the same in every quarter (no growth); angular momentum to 2×10⁻¹³.
- [DEMONSTRATED: `invariants.test.ts`] Moving binary at the P16 scale, 2×10⁶ s (≈ 3 binary periods): barycentric velocity exactly 0, angular momentum to 4×10⁻¹³, energy to 2×10⁻¹², separation stays circular to 1×10⁻¹⁰, ship Jacobi constant to 3×10⁻¹².
- [DEMONSTRATED: `rigid-body.test.ts`] Thrust off the centre of mass gives α = (p − c) × F / I; constant torque gives θ = ½αt² exactly; thrust on a spinning ship matches the analytic spiral with second-order convergence; specific force is the body-frame thrust over mass at any attitude.

**Clearance and contact:**
- [DEMONSTRATED: `contact.test.ts`] A 100 m/s pass into a 5 m asteroid (shorter than one step) is caught by the swept test at the exact contact time, point and speed; head-on rebound is e·v; an off-centre hit spins the ship; pushing into an object holds the hull at the surface with one impact event; one clearance event per pass, including for a spinning hull.
- [ASSUMED] Restitution 0.2 by default; bodies and asteroids are immovable in contact. Reason: they are 10⁸ kg and up against a 30 t ship; whether an impact is fatal is the game layer's call.
- [ASSUMED] Clearance beyond 5 km (+ the hull's reach) is measured from the centre of mass. Reason: cost; the error is at most the hull's size.
- [ASSUMED] An asteroid whose surface reaches a body's surface leaves the world (reported in `StepResult.absorbed`).
- [ASSUMED] A field's `modifyThrust` changes translation only; torque comes from the unmodified thrusts. Reason: the contract passes one total force; Inverse Wells (3B) may want otherwise.

**Determinism and performance:**
- [DEMONSTRATED: `determinism.test.ts`] On the mock encounter and on the P16 binary with 25 asteroids, 4,000 steps of a seeded random burn plan give identical state and event hashes on repeat; stepping matches bulk `run` bit for bit; a JSON save/restore part way continues identically; a different plan changes the hash; inputs are not mutated.
- [ASSUMED] Gravity for n = 2 and 2.5 is bit-identical across JavaScript engines (only correctly rounded IEEE operations); attitude trig is not.
- [DEMONSTRATED: `performance.test.ts`] 10⁵ steps of the moving binary plus a thrusting ship with hull clearance run in ≈ 15 ms (asserted < 100 ms). Measured separately: ≈ 50 ms with 10 asteroids, ≈ 120 ms with 30 (≈ 35 ns per asteroid per step, mostly gravity). `step` costs ≈ 0.8 µs per call (it builds a new world object).

## Open questions and uncertainty

- **GMAT tests** wait for 1D's merge (above).
- **Step size near close passes.** 1 s is accurate for the P16 scale. A pass within tens of km of a body, or a ship spinning at more than about 0.1 rad/s, would want a smaller step for clearance and contact accuracy. 2F decides the encounter's `dt` after seeing its geometry.
- **Asteroid count vs performance.** Above about 15 asteroids, 10⁵ steps take more than 100 ms. That is irrelevant at game speed (even 1000× warp is 1000 steps a second), but matters if anything wants long truth-side look-ahead with a big field. 2F chooses the field size.
- **Contact semantics** (what speed or impulse destroys the ship, whether glancing contact damages equipment) belong to 2F/2B.
