# S2 — Hardware, ship couplings and operation

Status: proposed, not approved. **[ASSUMED] applies to every selected rating, geometry, operating limit, wear coefficient and implementation rule in this document and the [JSON fragment](hardware-fragment.json).** `[ESTABLISHED: …]` labels mechanisms supported by the [precedents](precedents.md) or project bibliography; `[DEMONSTRATED: reference.py]` means arithmetic checked by [the reference script](../../../tools/studies/s2/reference.py), not a playable encounter or flight-qualified design.

## Recommendation and its limits

[ASSUMED] Install a **255 kg dorsal grapple-and-winch kit**, including one 500 m line and one 15 kg passive enclosing grapple. Capture a surveyed rail on the rotating structure from 20–50 m after matching its attachment-point velocity. Use controlled payout to take load, follow the line with real attitude control, and release the cassette at the ship. This is the smallest coherent architecture identified here, not a proved global mass minimum. It needs no EVA, penetrating projectile, powered target or novel physics. It does require compatible target geometry and a structural retrofit; it cannot be conjured out of the ship's present inventory.

[ASSUMED] The useful operating point is **6 kN tension**, with **8 kN working load**, **3 kW motor input**, and **4 kW brake dissipation while paying out**. A locked taut line consumes no continuous mechanical power, although control, observation and attitude keeping still do. The brake's 4 kW is a friction-power operating rating into finite thermal capacity, not a promise of indefinite rejection. Reel-in is for preparation and slow length management, not an unlimited ship-hauling engine.

[ASSUMED] Choose a 0.6 m maximum-envelope closing grapple with a 0.3 m clear jaw aperture around an exposed 0.08–0.25 m diameter rail with 0.6 m clear length. Its jaws must enclose and latch around that member; a ray hit or hook touching sheet metal is insufficient. Its assumed 60 J local compliance/crush capacity covers the 46.9 J head-only impact at 2.5 m/s, **not** the ship's capture energy. A visible latch witness and a gentle load test supply evidence of retention. Neither observation proves that a corroded rail can carry 8 kN. Target structural response/strength remains an S1/S3 truth parameter and a belief uncertainty.

[ASSUMED] Select a provisional **0.04 s** jaw-closing interval. Contact must retain the rail inside the capture volume throughout that interval, followed by a real closed-latch retention constraint. For a transverse pass through a 0.3 m aperture, the crude dwell screen `(aperture − rail diameter)/transverse speed` gives only 0.02 s for a 0.25 m rail at 2.5 m/s: that capture fails this screen despite meeting the separate impact-energy limit. The corresponding transverse-speed ceiling is 1.25 m/s before uncertainty/margin. S1/S3 must test contact/compliance/retention and derive the actual envelope; the stated maximum impact speed is not a promise of capture from every direction.

The capture alternatives and heritage are compared in [precedents.md](precedents.md). [ASSUMED] A harpoon adds material/penetration uncertainty; a net adds many contact points and wrapping dynamics. Both can be worthwhile later, but neither removes the need for the winch, brake, release and instrumentation. The present recommendation is a launched closing grapple, not an undocumented existing docking arm.

## Line, mount and occupied geometry in three dimensions

[ASSUMED] Use a nonconductive aramid-class braided line with jacket, effective diameter 6 mm, effective axial rigidity `EA = 1.0e6 N`, new minimum terminated strength 40 kN. These are proposed packaged properties, not values guaranteed by the material name. Knots, bends, termination efficiency, vacuum/temperature ageing and abrasion need qualification. The 8 kN working limit is an assumed factor-five margin to the new line's break rating. A 16 kN sacrificial connection is below the assumed 24 kN frame failure load; frame proof level is 12 kN. The weaker target, damaged line, side-loaded fairlead or snared appendage can fail first. Do not use 40 kN as the whole-system safe limit.

[ASSUMED] Body axes follow the contracts: +x nose, +y left, +z dorsal, origin fixed to structure. The working fairlead is at **(−0.35, 0, 3.20) m**, above the near-arrival COM station. Reinforcement ties to hull rings, not the thin pressure skin, solar hinge or radiator. The launcher points +z; ship attitude aims it. A swivel admits a 35° half-angle cone around +z. Normal operating alignment is within 2° of the ray from **current** COM through the fairlead. An out-of-cone line rubs or contacts the guide; it does not receive a free gimbal correction.

[ASSUMED] Stowed kit bounding box: x [−1.1, 0.4], y [−0.65, 0.65], z [1.7, 2.8] m. Deployed kit: same x/y, z up to 3.5 m. The frame moves 10 kg of its mass through 0.7 m (assembly centroid rises 0.1 m); launcher and release rise 0.7 m; sensor head rises 0.75 m. Interpolate actual extension over 8 s and retain partial extension on power loss. The kit protrudes 0.3 m above the current ±2.5 m hull even stowed. These are conservative boxes, not decorative dimensions that can be ignored in clearance.

[ASSUMED] Mast travel is permitted only before firing or after physical head recovery and an unloaded, safe line path. Its 960 J budget is for that internal motion; it cannot pull a 6 kN line through 0.75 m (4.5 kJ of mechanical work). Unexplained load during motion stops the actuator, and the actual mechanical work enters its energy ledger. The steady 120 W heat endpoint assumes zero net stored kinetic energy after an unloaded move, not that transient internal motion has no momentum.

[ASSUMED] Preserve all four ship configurations (`stowed`, `radiators`, `solar`, `allDeployed`); tether mast and line state are orthogonal states. Each configuration is the union of its actual 3D hull/appendages and the kit. Line geometry is a swept chain of 3 mm-radius capsules and a 0.3 m-radius head, not a 500 m enlargement of the rigid hull. A taut nominal dorsal line clears the coplanar wings; a slack loop or snag need not. Sweep every intermediate appendage/mast/line position; a change of configuration can strike the line. Do not stow a loaded mast. The pending physical 2G hull can invalidate these clearances and must be checked on integration.

[ASSUMED] Do **not** include the extended line/head as a rigid 500 m ship appendage. Split the cassette's 20 kg by actual paid length and the launcher's 15 kg head at firing. [DEMONSTRATED: reference.py] At 6 kN the ship's specific acceleration is about **0.196 m/s²**. The spring-fired head gives only **0.000979 m/s** ship recoil, too small to matter for route momentum, though contact/line events remain observable. Adding the kit lowers ideal full main-only Δv from 690.61 to 684.18 m/s, a 6.42 m/s cost; there is no propellant refill. The useful momentum gain must come from the moving structure, not from firing the head.

[DEMONSTRATED: reference.py] Exact finite-carrier launch kinetic energy at 2 m/s relative speed is 29.9853 J; the remaining 0.0147 J of the 30 J launch spring is allocated to launcher loss. Jaw closure has its separate 2 J spring and dissipates that energy in closure/contact. Recovered heads need both springs recocked. At the 50 m partial-payout release, 1 J cassette separation work gives about 0.295 m/s relative clearance speed and **0.000222 m/s** opposite ship recoil. This is too small to matter in the route but closes the release ledger; departing hardware remains in the collision scene.

## Capture and slack take-up are energy problems

[ESTABLISHED: S2-SEDS, S2-MXER] End-of-payout transients and compliant capture matter. `[ASSUMED]` Require observed target-point velocity matching before firing, with a proposed radial relative speed at ship load engagement no greater than 0.5 m/s. This is a design-envelope recommendation, not a magic game interlock on hidden velocity. Display the uncertain measured speed; truth contact remains geometric. Head impact speed must stay below its separate 2.5 m/s local qualification envelope. The closing rail must pass between the jaws during the finite latch interval; line payout and the moving head must be simulated before contact.

[DEMONSTRATED: reference.py] A 50 m elastic line with `k = EA/L = 20,000 N/m` and a rigid, infinitely massive target gives an undamped locked-line screening peak `v √(k m_eff)`: **12.37 kN at 0.5 m/s**, **49.49 kN at 2 m/s**. The infinite-target translational approximation ignores target compliance, both attachment lever arms and pre-existing tension; it is a screening example, not a conservative bound for every rotating capture. For two freely translating point masses, `1/m_eff = 1/m_ship + 1/m_target`; rotational attachment mobility adds `(r×n)^T I⁻¹(r×n)` terms. S1 owns the actual coupled calculation.

[DEMONSTRATED: reference.py] At 0.5 m/s, 3.83 kJ of relative radial kinetic energy requires at least 0.638 m of ideal constant-6-kN stopping payout and 2.55 s; initial brake dissipation is 3 kW. At 2 m/s, energy is 61.2 kJ and initial brake dissipation would be 12 kW, outside this kit's envelope. The minimum-distance calculation neglects force ramp, geometry change and elastic storage. Author an approach that can match velocity, never solve an arbitrarily fast flyby by silently reducing its energy.

[ASSUMED] The brake command sets resisting torque/slip, not a kinematic promise that line speed or tension instantly equals a demand. Motor speed/torque, line compliance, brake heat, available length and two-body motion decide the result. Zero extension means slack and zero tensile force; never let the rope push. The remaining reserve must exceed the forecast stopping payout plus uncertainty. Reaching 500 m while still moving is a hard-end transient, not normal “deployment complete.”

[ASSUMED] During launch the powered capstan is disengaged and the line peels from a low-inertia, stationary pack; the 1 m/s powered retrieval limit does not clamp launch payout. Specify a 3 m/s free-payout operating envelope and a **0.1 N mean drag design target** including guides/counter losses. At 50 m that would consume 5 J, with a further conservative 4 J allowance to accelerate 2 kg of line at 2 m/s, leaving about 21 J for head motion. This arithmetic does not prove a 50 m throw: drag, line dynamics and target motion require simulation and hardware testing. The head slows while paying out; if the real pack cannot meet this drag target, shorten the range or resize the launcher openly. A rotating storage drum with unbudgeted inertia is not this mechanism. Retrieval must repack the line consistently or the next throw's drag changes.

## Electrical and thermal coupling

[ASSUMED] Payload/main-B feeds drive, deployment, launcher and sensing. Essential 28 V feeds the 35 W controller and 50 W capacitor charger; include the existing 95% converter loss at its supplying PDU. Healthy stored energy and an essential decoder allow release/cut through payload loss. Total essential loss can defeat commands: the capacitor is not an invisible independent computer. Passive brake and overload link remain mechanical. Prioritize controller/release while attached, preserving existing essential air/control/recorder priorities; shed the optional motor first. Loss of its current must not disable release on another protected branch.

[DEMONSTRATED: reference.py] With the baseline arrival loads, no solar and separate buses, observation adds 66.84 W at source level. Reeling at 6 kN and 0.3 m/s exports 1,800 W mechanically, draws **2,500 W**, and deposits **700 W** in the drive. With controller/sensors total ship demand is **5,591 W** (A 2,851 W, B 2,740 W). High-rate recorder mode raises that to **5,659 W**. Maximum motor plus ion mode would ask B for **10,460 W**, exceeding its 6 kW fuel cell + 4 kW battery even though A retains spare generation; real shedding or a healthy commanded tie is needed. Electrical-source ramp, state of charge and thermal limits can still invalidate these steady cases.

[ASSUMED] Use `P_in = 100 + T |Ldot| / 0.75` for powered shortening, capped at 3 kW and 1 m/s no-load travel; achieved motion follows mechanics. At 6 kN maximum power permits only **0.3625 m/s**. Effective capstan radius 0.2 m means **1,200 N m** output torque. At negligible load, the 100 W overhead remains; loss of power stalls commanded retrieval rather than conjuring missing work. Lowering under load dissipates into the brake, with **no electrical regeneration** in this minimum design. Locked length with zero slip has zero `T Ldot` heat. Drum inertia and spin energy belong in S1's detailed spool integration; the simple rates here are quasistatic ratings.

[ASSUMED] Two local thermal nodes prevent a common-temperature shortcut from hiding a hot brake: drive 32,500 J/K (65 kg × 500 J/kg/K), brake/frame 60,000 J/K (100 kg × effective 600 J/kg/K). These capacities allocate existing kit mass, not additional mass. Drive links to service at 20 W/K with pumping; brake/frame at 25 W/K pumped or 5 W/K passive. Charge/control/sensors heat service. The service/external-loop/radiator path and its existing limits then apply; transfer is removed from the hot node and added once to service. Inactive pumps leave only the brake's 5 W/K passive path; drive active transfer stops. These links must be added on adoption, not inferred from node names.

[DEMONSTRATED: reference.py] A 60 s payout at 6 kN and 0.5 m/s dissipates **180 kJ**, raising the local brake/frame **3 K** adiabatically. At 4 kW from 295 K, its 330 K warning is reached in **525 s** without cooling. At 330 K against a 295 K service node, the pumped link carries only **875 W**; therefore 4 kW cannot be sustained indefinitely. Passive `C/K` is 12,000 s: a brief wait does not restore thermal capacity. Bulk service warming from 180 kJ alone would be just 0.045 K, too small to represent the local hazard. Additional fuel-cell generation creates its own heat per 1C; do not forget it or double-count exported winch work as onboard heat.

[ASSUMED] Brake friction authority scales linearly from full at 330 K to zero at 360 K; subsequent overheated friction damage persists. A 4 kW exceedance deposits the **actual** mechanical heat and may cause rapid fade; it is not clipped to 4 kW. Frame proof/failure and rope properties are also temperature/condition-dependent in a later qualified design. The minimum implementation retains the stated local temperature trip/fade and degraded break limits rather than claiming a calibrated material model.

## Wear, damage and calibration (A5)

[ASSUMED] Track continuous condition separately for rope material segments, drive, brake, gate and sensors. Store line wear by material coordinate so reeling does not swap a damaged portion for a healthy numeric average. For a minimum approximation use uniform health `h` and expose its limitation. Per segment use a cumulative damage increment:

`ΔD = |Δs|/2e6 + |Δs|(T/8000)^2/4e5 + 1e−8 Δt (T/8000)^4 + 0.1 × snagSlidingDistance`.

Here length is m, time s; bending/abrasion terms apply to material passing the guide, creep to loaded material, and snag damage only to contacted material. `h = max(0, 1−D)`, tensile break `40,000 h N`, axial rigidity `1e6(0.5+0.5h) N`, and advised working tension `8,000 h N`. The weak link stays 16 kN unless separately damaged; dynamic rupture occurs when **actual** tension exceeds the weakest actual limit. Stop recommending operation below `h=0.5`; no health threshold magically severs an unloaded rope. These coefficients are deliberate tuning assumptions, not an S–N curve or a physical service-life claim.

[DEMONSTRATED: reference.py] A uniform-health bookkeeping example of 1,000 m travel at 6 kN and 1,800 s loaded changes D by about **0.00191** (0.19%). Ordinary single-encounter cycling is too small to create dramatic decay; do not invent large fatigue damage for play. Two metres of sharp snag sliding adds 0.2 to the contacted segment under the assumed rule and is consequential. Exposed collision/cut damage and retained faults, rather than a short mission's ordinary fatigue, supply most immediate risk.

[ASSUMED] Drive wear increments with actual dissipated energy / 2e9 J; health reduces output torque and efficiency `η = 0.75(0.5+0.5h_drive)`. Brake wear follows dissipated energy / 2e9 J plus `1e−4 × max(T_brake−330,0) × Δt` while hot; reduce slip authority by health as well as temperature fade. Jam is a distinct mechanical state from electrical trip; reset clears only recoverable logic/trips, never broken gears, frayed line or expended hardware. Lattice damage uses the already fictional electrical profiles at actual kit locations; a passive rope does not receive an invented TSS high-voltage arc. `[FICTIONAL: baseline radiation mapping]` is separate from ordinary wear assumptions.

[ASSUMED] The player/model receives inspection, temperature, current, strain and quality evidence, not `h_truth`. A5 ages load-cell zero/scale, payout scale/slip, camera alignment and brake friction calibration with ship clock, use and observed condition drops; nominal hardware knowledge carries, fresh geometry/strength evidence does not. See the sensing specification for explicit ageing equations and missing/saturated-data handling.

## Failure modes and retained consequences

| Failure | Physical cause and consequence | What can be observed and done |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Miss | Head passes the rail or fails to close; it remains an independent tethered mass. Spool-end arrest can still snatch the ship. No momentum-transfer reward | Camera track, launch/payout pulses, no convincing load/visual retention. Brake gently, avoid head return impact, rewind only a clear path; otherwise cut and lose that inventory |
| Snag | Line touches a broken truss, hull or appendage; creates a new contact/load path, friction, local wear, possible wrapping and angular impulse. Original endpoint distance is no longer line path length | Payout/shaft disagreement, rising load, line-bearing change, IMU/gyro response, visible contact when observable. Stop reel, unload if possible, release/cut. Do not teleport a snagged line through the obstacle |
| Slack snap-back / take-up | Slack loop takes up at finite relative speed; sudden extension generates a tension wave and whip. Can exceed weak link or target capacity despite acceptable steady load | High-rate load/IMU burst or saturation, line motion and later recoil. Preserve the burst or explicit acquisition gap. Controlled payout reduces load; a stiff locked stop can fail even at the 0.5 m/s nominal approach |
| Recoil on release | Releasing an unstrained ideal connector introduces **no extra velocity impulse**. Already-acquired ship velocity remains. Stored strain and remaining gate contact can deliver a finite transient; freed line/cassette can strike the ship | Predict coasting from estimated pre-release velocity; retain command, gate indication and subsequent accelerometer/camera evidence. Unload first when geometry permits. Do not add a scripted “slingshot kick” at the release timestamp |
| Cut under load | Severs continuity with stored strain still present; free line recoils toward its remaining attachment and local end can whip. Cut may leave line entangled around the ship, so detachment is not guaranteed merely by severing one point | Fired-initiator switch proves actuation, not a successful sever. Confirm unloading and camera motion; second initiator if necessary. Track lost mass and debris, cutter inventory and residual stowed line |
| Broken winch | Electrical open/trip stops motor; passive brake may still hold/slip. Seized level-wind/drum can also block payout, while stripped drive may leave only the independent brake. Cannot meet a changed length plan | Separate current, encoder motion, tension and heat. Isolate, use surviving brake/release/cutter and fly the current physical state. Never reset to healthy; a cut may be the only escape |

[DEMONSTRATED: reference.py] At 6 kN and 500 m, extension is 3 m and elastic energy **9 kJ**. If all that energy were put into the 30.6 t ship it would correspond to only 0.767 m/s; if concentrated into the 20 kg line it corresponds to 30 m/s. These are separate energy ceilings, not simultaneous predicted recoil speeds. The line's longitudinal wave speed from these assumptions is 5,000 m/s (0.1 s end-to-end). A 1 s ship step or 10 Hz average does not resolve cut/whip peaks. S1 must select converged substeps/contact treatment or explicitly bound unresolved transients; a massless line cannot honestly claim to predict local whip damage.

[ASSUMED] Best implementation: coupled massive flexible line, finite-contact grapple/gate, real spool mass flux, local wear/temperature and sensed uncertainty. A smaller release-ready build can use a unilateral elastic line with controlled payout and deterministic event splits, but must keep whole-line collision checks and admit it cannot predict wrapping, local whip or distributed abrasion. If those omitted effects are needed for an authored geometry, defer that geometry; do not replace them with random loss rolls. The smaller build still needs the full physical inventory, two-body reaction, energy ledger and honest sensing.
